How it works

Aim at the sticker. That's it.

If the sticker has a Wi‑Fi QR code, it's read the moment it appears in the camera. If not, tap Read and the app finds the network name and password in the printed text.

  • Or pick a photo of the sticker from your gallery
  • Understands labels like SSID, Network key or WPA2 key
  • Even when labels and values are in separate columns
Double-checked

Fixes what the camera got wrong.

Tiny print is easy to misread. If it read MOVISTAR_I234 and MOVISTAR_1234 is nearby, the app uses the real one and tells you. Not the right password? Pick it from everything it read.

  • Shows when the network is nearby
  • Edit the name or password before connecting
  • Copy the password in one tap
Connect

One tap, and you're online.

Tap Connect and Android shows its own "Save this network?" dialog. Accept, and your phone joins the Wi‑Fi by itself.

  • Android always asks before saving
  • On older Android, the password is copied and Wi‑Fi settings open

FAQ

Questions? Answers.

Can it get into other people's Wi‑Fi?

No. It's made for the sticker on your own router or a network you're allowed to use. It only reads what's printed on the label you point it at.

Why does it ask for my location?

Only while reading text, so it can see nearby networks and correct a misread name. Android requires this permission to scan for networks. If you deny it, the app still works, just without name correction.

Does it need an internet connection?

No. QR codes and printed text are read on your phone, offline. Nothing you scan is sent anywhere.

Which Android version do I need?

Saving a network from the app needs Android 11 or newer. On older versions, and for WEP or enterprise networks that Android doesn't let apps save, the app copies the password and opens Wi‑Fi settings so you can paste it.

What if it reads the password wrong?

You can edit it before connecting, or tap "Pick from the text read" and choose the right piece of text.

Which languages are available?

21 languages. The app follows your Android language.
